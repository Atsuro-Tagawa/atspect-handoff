en_bio: Born in Asuka Village, Nara Prefecture. He graduated from the Department of Architecture, Nara Prefectural Yoshino Technical School, in 1940, and worked at the Tokyo branch office of Obayashi Corporation. He also studied at the Department of Architecture, Waseda Higher Technical School. After the war he ran the Ishii Construction Firm independently, and in 1956 he opened the Biken Architectural Design Office. In 1999 he became a part-time faculty member at the Hyogo Prefectural Awaji Landscape Planning and Horticulture Academy. He designed Meganeyama House 1, "Kaiki Soan" (his own residence, Nishinomiya, Hyogo Prefecture; completed 1976), Ten to Chi no Ie (Sakai, Osaka Prefecture; completed 1974), and Meganeyama House 8 (Nishinomiya, Hyogo Prefecture; completed 1983), among others. He received the Architectural Institute of Japan Prize for fiscal year 1986 for the "series of houses at Meganeyama," and in 1987 received the 12th Yoshida Isoya Prize (Honorable Mention) for Meganeyama House 8. In 2002 he received the 4th JIA 25-Year Award for his own residence, Meganeyama House 1 (Kaiki Soan). He died in 2007 at the age of 85.
en_viewpoint: An architect who became independent after working at Obayashi Corporation, and who continued residential design work centered on the Meganeyama area of Nishinomiya, Hyogo Prefecture. He received the Architectural Institute of Japan Prize for fiscal year 1986 for the "series of houses at Meganeyama," which includes his own residence, "Kaiki Soan."
