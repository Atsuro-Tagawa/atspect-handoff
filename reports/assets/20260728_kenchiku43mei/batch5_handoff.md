en_bio: Born in Kyoto City. After attending Aoyama Gakuin Junior High School, he graduated from the Design Department of Kyoto Higher Technical School (present-day Kyoto Institute of Technology) in 1928. In 1935 he began working on architectural design after taking on the residence of his brother-in-law, the Japanese-style painter Kondo Koichiro. He designed the former Shinwa Bank Head Office Main Building (Sasebo, Nagasaki Prefecture; completed 1967, extended 1969), the former Shinwa Bank Head Office Kaishokan (same location; completed 1975), and the Shoto Museum of Art (Shibuya Ward, Tokyo; completed 1980, opened 1981, designed by the Seiichi Shirai Institute), among others. He received the Architectural Institute of Japan Prize for fiscal year 1968 and the Mainichi Art Award for fiscal year 1969, both for the Shinwa Bank Head Office Main Building, and received the Takamura Kotaro Prize in 1961 and the Japan Art Academy Prize in 1980. He died in 1983 at the age of 78.
