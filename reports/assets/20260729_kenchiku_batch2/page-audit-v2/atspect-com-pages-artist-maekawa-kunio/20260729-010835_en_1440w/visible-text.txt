Biography
Born in Niigata City. In 1909 his family moved to Tokyo when his father was transferred for work. He entered the Department of Architecture, Faculty of Engineering, Tokyo Imperial University, in 1925 and graduated in 1928. On the night of his graduation ceremony he traveled to France, where he studied at the atelier of Le Corbusier, whose writings had influenced him since his student days. He returned to Japan in 1930 and, from August of that year, worked at the architectural office of Antonin Raymond; he left the office in 1935 and established the Maekawa Kunio Architectural Design Office. He worked on projects including the Kimura Industry Research Institute (Hirosaki, Aomori Prefecture; completed 1932), the Kanagawa Prefectural Library and Music Hall (Yokohama; completed 1954), and the International House of Japan main building (Minato Ward, Tokyo; completed 1955, co-designed with Junzo Sakakura and Junzo Yoshimura). He received the Architectural Institute of Japan Prize on multiple occasions, the UIA Auguste Perret Prize in 1963, the Architectural Institute of Japan Grand Prize in 1968, and the Tokyo Metropolitan Cultural Award in 1985. He also served as president of the Japan Institute of Architects. He died in 1986 at the age of 81.
Legacy
An architect who studied under Le Corbusier in France and, after returning to Japan, gained experience at the office of Antonin Raymond, playing a role in the practice of modern architecture in Japan. Beginning with the Kimura Industry Research Institute, he worked on a wide range of buildings, including libraries, concert halls, and cultural facilities. Some of his works have been passed down to the present through renovation and preservation work.
Selected Works
Kimura Industry Research Institute (Hirosaki, Aomori Prefecture; completed 1932; extant; nationally designated Important Cultural Property, designated 2021), Kanagawa Prefectural Library and Music Hall (Yokohama, Kanagawa Prefecture; completed 1954; closed for renovation; prefecturally designated Important Cultural Property, designated 2021), International House of Japan main building (Minato Ward, Tokyo; completed 1955, co-designed with Junzo Sakakura and Junzo Yoshimura; extant; nationally Registered Tangible Cultural Property, registered 2006)
Collections
1 nationally designated Important Cultural Property (Kimura Industry Research Institute), 1 nationally Registered Tangible Cultural Property (International House of Japan main building), 1 prefecturally designated Important Cultural Property (Kanagawa Prefectural Library and Music Hall). A dedicated memorial museum or archive has not been confirmed.
