Biography
Born in Fukagawa, Tokyo (present-day Koto Ward, Tokyo). He studied under Toshikata Sano in the Department of Architecture at the College of Engineering, Tokyo Imperial University, and graduated in 1907. After returning to the university, he became a professor in 1921 and served as president of Tokyo Imperial University from 1943 to 1945. On the campus of the University of Tokyo he left a series of buildings, including Yasuda Auditorium (completed 1925, designed together with Hideto Kishida), Faculty of Law Building No. 3 (completed 1927), the main building of the former First Higher School (now the clock tower, completed 1933), and Faculty of Letters and Law Building No. 1 (completed 1935); his style later came to be known as "Uchida Gothic." He also served multiple terms as president of the Architectural Institute of Japan, and in 1951 became chairman of the Cultural Properties Protection Committee. In December 1972, the same year he received the Order of Culture, he died in Tokyo of acute pneumonia.
Legacy
An architect notable as an educator, who oversaw the administration of the university as president of Tokyo Imperial University in addition to his own design work. The cluster of buildings he designed on the University of Tokyo campus, known for their unified style called "Uchida Gothic," formed the backbone of the campus and continue to be used today as registered tangible cultural properties. Having studied under Toshikata Sano and having in turn trained many successors himself, he can be considered one of the figures who embodied modern architectural education in Japan.
