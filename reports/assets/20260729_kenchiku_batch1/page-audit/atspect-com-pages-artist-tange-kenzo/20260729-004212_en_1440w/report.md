Biography
Born in Osaka (some sources give Ehime Prefecture as his birthplace). He graduated from the Department of Architecture, Faculty of Engineering, Tokyo Imperial University, in 1938, gained experience at the architectural office of Kunio Maekawa, and then pursued a career in teaching and research at his alma mater. He became an associate professor in 1946 and a professor in 1963, training many architects until his retirement in 1974. In 1961 he established his own design organization and worked on projects including the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum (completed 1955), the Kagawa Prefectural Government Office East Building (completed 1958), and the Yoyogi National Gymnasium (completed 1964). He was also involved in organizing the architectural profession, serving as the founding president of the New Japan Architects Association, the predecessor of today's Japan Institute of Architects (JIA). He received the Order of Culture in 1980 and, in 1987, became the first Japanese national to win the Pritzker Prize. He died in 2005 at the age of 91.
Legacy
One of the central figures who brought postwar Japanese architecture onto the international stage. In works such as the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum and the Yoyogi National Gymnasium, he repeatedly sought to translate elements of traditional Japanese design into the structure and form of modern architecture. He is also known for his long career teaching at the University of Tokyo, where he trained many architects who went on to represent the next generation.
