Biography
Born in Oita City. He graduated from the Department of Architecture, Faculty of Engineering, University of Tokyo, in 1954, studied in Kenzo Tange's laboratory, and completed his doctoral studies at the University of Tokyo Graduate School in 1963, the same year he established his own design organization, Arata Isozaki & Associates. He left numerous works in Japan and abroad, including the Gunma Museum of Modern Art (completed 1974), the Art Tower Mito (completed 1990), the former Oita Prefectural Library (now Art Plaza, completed 1966), and the Tsukuba Center Building (completed 1983), and also served as a visiting professor at universities overseas. He received the Architectural Institute of Japan Prize and, in 2019, the Pritzker Prize. He died in 2022 of old age at his home in Naha, Okinawa Prefecture, at the age of 91.
Legacy
An architect who continually expanded his field of activity from public buildings and museums to large-scale projects overseas, working both in Japan and abroad. His projects ranged widely in region and scale, from the former Oita Prefectural Library (now Art Plaza) in his hometown of Oita to the Museum of Contemporary Art, Los Angeles. He was also an active voice in architectural criticism and theory, consistently positioning Japanese architecture within international debate.
