Maki Fumihiko
Architect
Life1928–2024 ／ FromTokyo

Biography
Born in Tokyo. He graduated from the Department of Architecture, Faculty of Engineering, University of Tokyo in 1952, then studied at the Cranbrook Academy of Art and the Harvard Graduate School of Design in the United States, completing his master's degree in 1954. He gained teaching and practical experience in the United States, including serving as an associate professor at Washington University in St. Louis, before returning to Japan in 1965 and founding Maki and Associates. He served as a professor in the Department of Architecture, Faculty of Engineering, University of Tokyo from 1979 to 1989. He left works in Japan and abroad, including Hillside Terrace (Shibuya, Tokyo; built in stages from 1969 to 1992), Spiral (Minato, Tokyo; completed 1985), and Makuhari Messe (Chiba City, Chiba Prefecture; completed 1989). He was also involved in the Metabolism movement of the 1960s. He received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 1993 and was named a Person of Cultural Merit in 2013. He died in 2024 at the age of 95.
Legacy
An architect who, after architectural education in Japan and abroad, continued to create tranquil public spaces within the city. Rather than being completed at once as a single building, Hillside Terrace was a townscape cultivated in stages over more than twenty years — an attempt that incorporated the passage of time itself into the design. He is also known for maintaining his own restrained style even as his work intersected with that of his Metabolist contemporaries.
Selected Works
Hillside Terrace (Tokyo, Shibuya Ward; completed in stages 1969–1992; extant), Spiral (Tokyo, Minato Ward; completed 1985; extant), Makuhari Messe (Chiba City, Chiba Prefecture; completed 1989; extant)
Collections
No cultural property designation could be confirmed this time. Materials are held at the Maki Fumihiko Archive (Keio University Shonan Fujisawa Campus).
