Seike Kiyoshi
Architect
Life1918–2005 ／ FromKyoto Prefecture
Link to this artist's page
Copy link
QR code
Biography
Born in Kyoto. He spent his boyhood in Kobe. He graduated from the Architecture Department of the Tokyo School of Fine Arts in 1941 and from Tokyo Institute of Technology in 1943, studying under architect Yoshiro Taniguchi during his student years. He remained at Tokyo Institute of Technology, serving as research assistant and associate professor, and became a professor in 1962. He later also held a concurrent professorship at Tokyo University of the Arts, where he served as dean of the Faculty of Fine Arts. He is known for the design of small houses, including his representative work "My House" (Watashi no Ie, completed 1954, designated a Registered Tangible Cultural Property in 2017), as well as the Dr. Mori House (1951) and the Associate Professor Saito House (1952). He served as president of the Architectural Institute of Japan from 1981 and trained many architects, including Kazuo Shinohara, Shoji Hayashi, and Masako Hayashi. He died in 2005 at the age of 86.
Legacy
A residential architect who pursued quality of living within limited sites and budgets. His representative work, "My House," is a single-story house of just 51 square meters, yet it is known for its skillful design of the passage of light and air, demonstrating the possibilities of the postwar Japanese "small house." As an educator, he also built the "Seike Laboratory" at Tokyo Institute of Technology, training many residential architects of the next generation, including Kazuo Shinohara.
Selected Works
"My House" (Watashi no Ie) (Tokyo, Ota Ward; completed 1954; extant; Registered Tangible Cultural Property, registered 2017), Kyushu Institute of Technology Memorial Auditorium (Kitakyushu, Fukuoka Prefecture; completed 1960; extant)
Collections
One Registered Tangible Cultural Property (My House, designated 2017). A dedicated memorial museum or archive has not been confirmed.
Art Groups
